# Theodore Roosevelt — Unnumbered Executive Orders: Working Catalog

*The enumerable worklist of TR's **unnumbered** executive orders — the orders that fall
outside the numbered 141–1050 series already deep-dived in `TR_Executive_Actions_Research.md`.*

## Where this comes from

Source: **Clifford L. Lord (ed.), *List and Index of Presidential Executive Orders,
Unnumbered Series, 1789–1941*** (Historical Records Survey, Work Projects Administration,
Newark, N.J., [1943]). This is the standard published catalog of the unnumbered orders; it
lists each order by date with a one-line description and citations to where the text can be
found. Public domain (US). Retrieved from the Library of Congress
(item 43013029). The OCR text is saved in `sources/` (`43013029.text.txt`), and
`sources/TR_unnumbered_raw_1901-1909.txt` holds a raw transcription of the Roosevelt pages.
*(Note, 2026-10-12: `43013029.pdf` is not in `sources/` in this copy of the project. The
page-image readings below come from earlier passes and cannot be re-checked from these files
until the PDF is restored.)*

TR's unnumbered orders occupy **book pages 186–204** of the volume (PDF pages 208–226;
PDF page = book page + 22). The run starts at the foot of p. 186 (Sept. 20, 1901) and ends
at Dec. 1, 1908 on p. 204, and **Lord lists 126 Roosevelt entries**. Pages 204–207 continue
with Taft. *(Superseded: a first sweep of the OCR put the run at pp. 187–207 and counted
"roughly 75–80 entries," matching the old "~75 unnumbered EOs" guess of 1,081 APP-credited
orders minus 1,006 numbered rows. Both the page range and the count were wrong.)*

## Method & accuracy note

The Lord volume is a **two-column** list (a column of dates beside a column of
descriptions), and the 1943 OCR scrambles the two columns together — naive text parsing
mispairs dates and descriptions. **Every entry in this catalog is therefore transcribed by
reading the scanned page images directly**, not from the raw OCR. Book page numbers are
recorded so any entry can be re-checked against `sources/43013029.pdf`.

Citations use Lord's abbreviations: **X Richardson** = Richardson, *Messages and Papers of
the Presidents*, vol. X; **CSR** = Civil Service Rules (annual); **GLO** = General Land
Office report; **CIA** = Commissioner of Indian Affairs report; **Kappler** = *Indian
Affairs: Laws and Treaties*; **IR** = Indian Reservations; **MR** = Military Reservations
compilations; **CFR** = Code of Federal Regulations; a bare **(EO ####)** is a *later*
numbered order that amended, enlarged, or superseded the unnumbered one — not a number for
the order itself. Lord's key says so in terms: "EO" followed by a number marks "a reference
in the Order having that number to the unnumbered Order here listed above such a citation."
A citation without parentheses points to a printed text; one in parentheses points only to
a later mention. A date in brackets is Lord's own inference.

## Reconciliation against the numbered series (completed 2026-10-12)

**Result (final, 2026-10-12).** Lord's 126 Roosevelt entries contain **105 distinct unnumbered orders**. Twenty-one entries are numbered documents that Lord lists a second time.

*How it got here.* The first pass on 2026-10-12 found 14 certain duplicates, 5 probable ones and 2 undeterminable entries, for a best estimate of 106 (range 105–107, ceiling 112). Later the same day the seven open identifications were all confirmed from Lord's own numbered-series volume (*Presidential Executive Orders, Numbered 1–8030*, 1944, vol. 1, Internet Archive `PresidentialExecutiveOrdersV1`) and period printings — see the notes on nos. 18, 101, 106, 107, 109, 110 and 120 and research entry #114. 126 − 21 = **105**.

**Method.** Every entry was compared with all 1,006 numbered orders (EOs 141–1050, from
`executive_actions_index.md`, which follows Wikipedia's *List of executive actions by
Theodore Roosevelt*) and all 407 proclamations (465–871).

- **Date match.** Each entry was checked for any numbered order or proclamation on the same
  date, then within ±10 days.
- **Subject match.** Every place name and subject keyword in the 126 entries was searched
  across all 1,413 numbered titles, with no date limit.
- **Reading the candidates.** Each candidate was read against Lord's own citation key (above)
  and the research file's deep dives. These cover entries #22, #44, #77–#106 and #110–#114.
- **Outside checks.** Three spot checks were made on 2026-10-12: the American Presidency
  Project texts of Proclamation 503 and of the Feb. 5, 1902 Prince Henry order, and
  Wikipedia's rows for the candidate EOs.

In the tables below, **"No match."** means that no numbered order or proclamation matched
the entry, either by date (±10 days) or by subject keyword. A parenthesized "(EO ####)" was **never** treated as an identification on its own, because it
is a back-reference. A match was scored **B** only on same date plus same subject, or on a
finding already settled in the research file. It was scored **B?** when the subject was
identical but the date differed by days, or when the date was Lord's bracketed inference.

**Residual uncertainty, as first stated** *(items 1–3 were resolved later on 2026-10-12 — all seven entries are numbered orders; item 4's Oklahoma caution still stands, and could lower the count to 103)*:
1. **No. 18 (Prince Henry, Feb. 5, 1902)** may be EO 162 (Jan. 24, 1902) under a different
   date. EO 162's text was not seen.
2. **No. 120 (Aug. 12, 1907, Civil Service Rules II/X)** shares its date with EO 679, which
   classifies an individual, A. J. Robinson. The two are probably different instruments, but
   EO 679's text was not seen.
3. **The five B? entries** rest on identical subjects rather than identical dates. Each is
   strongly supported, but each wants the RG 11 originals or Lord's numbered-series volume
   (1944) to settle it.
4. **Entries are not orders.** Lord warns that a same-day cluster "may have been effected by a
   group of individual Orders or, more probably, by a single Order."
   - The three Jan. 20, 1902 Oklahoma land entries (nos. 15–17) may be one order. If so, the
     order count falls by up to two more, to as low as **104** (or 103 if no. 120 also nets
     out).
   - The Mar. 21, 1905 trio is no longer a puzzle: all three are numbered (310-B, 321-A, 321-F).
5. **The numbered list itself** is Wikipedia's transcription. A numbered order missing from
   it, or misdated in it, would hide a match.
6. **The pre-numbering stretch is clean.** No numbered order exists before EO 141 of
   Oct. 31, 1901, and no proclamation besides 465 and 466 falls in 1901. Nos. 1–6 therefore
   cannot be duplicates.

**Knock-on figure (superseded — the final figure is 105 unnumbered, 1,111 executive orders, 1,518 documents).** 1,006 numbered + **106** unnumbered = **1,112 executive orders**. With
407 proclamations that makes **1,519 documents**: a range of 1,518–1,520 on the 105–107
range, and 1,525 at the 112 ceiling. These replace the "125 / 1,538" ceilings in the
research-file header. Suggested replacement wording is in `RECONCILIATION_REPORT.md`.

**Lord lists 120 TR entries (Nov 1901 – Dec 1908); 117 are distinct unnumbered orders.** Three
of the 120 (‡, all in 1907–1908) correspond to **numbered** orders already deep-dived in the
research file and are therefore not separate unnumbered orders:
- no. 110 (Jan 8, 1907, "U.S.S." ship designation) **= numbered EO 549** — research entry #91;
- no. 118 (July 1, 1908, Deschutes National Forest) and no. 119 (July 1, 1908, Fremont National
  Forest) **= numbered orders** of the July 1, 1908 forest reorganization — research entries #99–#102.

So the distinct unnumbered corpus is **117 orders**. The corpus effectively ends with the Dec 1,
1908 Round Top/Sugar Loaf reservation; every entry after it in the volume is dated 1909 and
belongs to Taft (a long run of "Temporary Power Site Withdrawals").

Once the full catalog is transcribed, these become the new deep-dive worklist: each gets an
8-field entry in `TR_Executive_Actions_Research.md` under the same proportional-depth rule
(most are routine administrative orders — Navy pay adjustments, land-district transfers,
half-mast orders, civil-service eligibilities — and get honest, compact treatment).
