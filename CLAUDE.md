# Executive Actions — working notes for Claude

Every executive order and proclamation Theodore Roosevelt signed as president (1901-09-14 → 1909-03-04), one record per document, served as a static JSON API + explorer + widgets + 114 stories at executiveactions.labs.trlibrary.com (GitHub Pages, built by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`; repo Theodore-Roosevelt-Presidential-Library/ExecutiveActions). Sibling project: Day by Day (`daybyday.labs.trlibrary.com`), which each record links to.

## Non-negotiables
- Historical accuracy comes first. Never invent a fact, date, name, number, citation or quotation. If something cannot be confirmed from the sources in this repo or the research entries, leave it out or mark it uncertain (a `date_note`, `title_note`, or a story's `open_questions`).
- Roosevelt's words are quoted verbatim with their source named (speech, letter, message, document and date), or not at all. Quotations from documents in the corpus are copied exactly, including original spelling, `[SEAL.]` and `[sic]`.
- Never present unverified text as verified. Only texts collated against a named primary printing or image get `kind: "collated"` and `verified: true`. Statutes digitizations and Wikisource transcriptions stay `verified: false`.
- Corrections go in `data/corrections.json`, each with a `why` that names its source. Never edit `data/base.jsonl` (it is regenerated by `parse_sources.py`) or the built `data/documents.jsonl` by hand.
- Stories add no facts beyond their research entries. A research entry's ⚠️ VERIFY flag is never stated as fact; it becomes an `open_questions` line.
- Summaries describe what the document did, based only on its title, Lord's description, the margin notes and its text. No outside history, motives or significance claims.
- Library voice: friendly, plain, specific, warm; a knowledgeable park ranger, not a professor. Minimal we/our/us/I. No museum-speak or marketing language. Nonpartisan: never frame Roosevelt's actions as belonging to a present-day side.
- "Buttes," never "mesa," except in proper nouns (Battlement Mesa Forest Reserve, Black Mesa).
- Never solve CAPTCHAs or get around access controls when fetching sources. Stop and report instead.
- Never commit `private/`. It holds the research file, the Statutes XML (a symlink), the research entries and the enrichment inputs.

| 6 | enrichment (worker, `enrich_brief.md`) | `private/enrich_in/NN.jsonl` | `data/enrich/NN.jsonl` | **yes** |

| 8 | `build_dataset.py` | all of `data/` (+ `private/TR_Executive_Actions_Research.md` for entry links) | `documents.jsonl/.csv`, `stories.json`, `stats.json` | optional |

GitHub Actions runs only step 9, from committed data. Steps 2, 6 and 7 need private inputs. Step 8 runs without `private/`, but then about 42 records lose story links that come from research-entry headings; don't commit a `documents.jsonl` rebuilt that way unless you mean to. Re-running `match_statutes.py` prints a validation report (unmatched, date differences, weak matches); read it before accepting a new map.

## Common tasks

**Add a correction.** Add or extend the document's key in `data/corrections.json`:
- `date`, `title`, `summary`, `states`, `people`, `action` replace the value (date and title changes also leave a "Corrected from …" note on the record);
- `date_note`, `title_note` add a note without changing the value;
- `why` is required for every replacement and names the source (Statutes volume and page, report and page, newspaper and date).
Then rebuild. If a fact is only probable, use a note, not a replacement.

**Add or edit a story.** Follow `pipeline/story_brief.md`. Write `data/stories/NNN.md` (zero-padded research entry number) with front matter `number, slug, title, dek, documents, related_documents, date_start, date_end, themes, places, open_questions`, then the sections At a glance, Background, Historical context, Political impact, Legacy, Sources. Use only document ids that exist in `data/base.jsonl`. Link other stories as `[title](story:NN)` and documents as `[label](doc:proc-563)`. Rebuild.

**Add a collated text.** Only when the text has been checked word for word against a named primary printing (a Statutes page image, a signed original, a period official printing). Add it to `data/stories/texts/NNN.json` under the story it belongs to: `{"proc-476": {"paragraphs": [...], "collated_against": "32 Stat. 2005–2006 (GovInfo page images), 2026-10-12", "notes": "..."}}`. Copy exactly. The build then replaces any machine text for that document and marks it `verified: true`.

**Re-pair a proclamation with its Statutes printing.** Add `{"proc-NNN": {"volume", "page", "date", "contains", "why"}}` to `data/statutes_overrides.json` (`contains` is a word that must appear in the printed text), then rerun `match_statutes.py` and step 8.

**Change summaries or categories.** Edit the document's line in `data/enrich/NN.jsonl` under the rules in `pipeline/enrich_brief.md`, or use `corrections.json` for a sourced fix to a summary.

## Rebuild and test

```bash
python3 pipeline/build_dataset.py && python3 pipeline/build_api.py
python3 -m http.server -d site 8000
```

Then check with Playwright (Chromium at `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium`) that these load without console errors at desktop and 390px widths: `index.html`, a proclamation with Statutes text (`document.html?id=proc-563`), an EO with Wikisource text (`document.html?id=eo-1003`), a Lord duplicate (`document.html?id=uo-018`), a story (`story.html?n=1`) and `widget.html`. The workflow also checks that `site/api/v1/index.json`, `documents.json`, `documents/proc-563.json`, `documents/eo-1003.json`, `on-this-day/02-29.json` and `site/CNAME` exist.

## Layout
`sources/` research catalogs · `data/` canonical dataset · `pipeline/` scripts and worker briefs · `site/` pages, `assets/`, `widgets/executiveactions.js` (`site/api/` and `site/data/` are generated and gitignored) · `docs/` schema and methods · `private/` research inputs (gitignored).
