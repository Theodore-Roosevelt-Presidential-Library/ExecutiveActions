## At a glance
Clifford Lord's index of unnumbered executive orders lists fifty-two Roosevelt entries from September 1901 through December 1902. Two are numbered orders that Lord lists a second time, so fifty unnumbered orders remain. Nearly all are administrative: land offices, reservations at home and overseas, Navy pay, individual civil-service cases, and a few ceremonies of state. Two small ones point forward, to a Nebraska tree nursery and to the Aleutian Islands. *(For what the unnumbered orders are and how they're counted, see [The Unnumbered Executive Orders](story:110).)*

## Background
**The first six.** Roosevelt was sworn in on September 14, 1901. His first unnumbered orders came in a quick scatter over the next seven weeks.

- **September 20:** He enlarged the Fort Sill Military Reservation in Comanche County, Oklahoma Territory, by 893.07 acres "for the purpose of locating thereon certain Apache prisoners of war." These were the Chiricahua, held by the Army since Geronimo's surrender in 1886 and still prisoners fifteen years later.
- **September 23:** He restored two sections of Colorado land to the public domain out of the Battlement Mesa Forest Reserve. Richardson prints it as "Black Mesa," a reserve that was actually in Arizona Territory. The township described lies in Garfield County, Colorado, and the Forest Service's chronologies record the order as a Battlement Mesa elimination.
- **October 1:** He commissioned Thaddeus S. Sharretts, a member of the Board of General Appraisers, to help settle "the conversion into specific duties … of all ad valorem duties on imports into China." This carried out the tariff article of the Boxer Protocol, the revenue meant to fund the indemnity.
- **October 15:** He fixed ratings and pay for the enlisted men of the Navy's Commissary Branch, effective the following January 1.
- **October 30:** He reserved Harbor Island and three islets in Sitka Harbor for the Revenue Cutter Service.
- **November 9:** He set apart for naval use all United States land in the Philippine provinces of Zambales and Bataan. The Olongapo station on Subic Bay was built there the next year.

**Land and the West.** The largest group reorganized the public-land offices. On January 20, 1902, three Oklahoma Territory districts were rebuilt at once: Oklahoma City lost land to El Reno and Mangum, and Perry and Enid were abolished and folded into Guthrie and Kingfisher. On April 2, 1902, the whole Territory of Alaska became a single Juneau Land District, and the Circle, Sitka, and Yukon districts, with their offices at Rampart City, Sitka, and St. Michael, were closed. Kansas and Washington district lines were redrawn, and Arizona's surveyor-general moved from Tucson to Phoenix.

**Reservations at home and abroad.** A steady stream of orders set apart land for federal use:

- lighthouse sites at Point Dume on the California coast (reserved twice, in nearly identical words) and on San Nicolas Island
- Weather Bureau and Customs tracts carved from the abandoned Fort Yuma reservation
- a Navy target range in Kitsap County, Washington
- naval reservations on Culebra, off Puerto Rico, and on Adak and Kiska in the Aleutians
- a quarantine site on Miraflores Island in San Juan Harbor
- a coal depot on Amaknak Island, Alaska
- more ground for Fort William H. Seward at Haines, Alaska, to take in a clay deposit for road-making

In the Philippines, these orders began building a network of bases by executive act. The Manila Bay reservations of April 11, 1902 took in Mariveles and Calumpan Point, the two headlands at the bay's entrance. The Malaga reservation on Samar followed in November, and at month's end the Olongapo Naval Station. Olongapo isn't on Manila Bay at all; it sits around the Bataan peninsula on Subic Bay.

**The Navy and the civil service.** Several orders set enlisted Navy pay and discipline: seventy-five cents a month extra for good conduct, extra pay for the Petty Officers' School certificate and two dollars for the seaman-gunner rating, a pay scale for mess attendants, and rules for early discharge. Others made named employees eligible for appointment, transfer, or reinstatement (Albert H. Clark, William B. Dunlany, Ellen W. Fitzsimmons, John T. Heck), and one gave honorably discharged Civil War veterans preference in appointment and retention.

**Indians, and the ceremonies of state.** Two orders added land to Pueblos in New Mexico Territory: San Felipe on June 13 and Nambe on September 4. Lord says these "established" the reservations, but both Pueblos already held their land under Spanish grants confirmed by United States patents in 1864; the orders added land next to it. The White Mountain (San Carlos) reservation in Arizona was diminished, and the Greenville Indian school reservation in California was enlarged.

A handful were ceremonial: flags at half-mast for former Agriculture Secretary J. Sterling Morton and for the British ambassador, Lord Pauncefote; and federal workers in Washington excused for the dedication of the Spanish-American War memorial and, on May 17, for the reburial at Arlington of General William S. Rosecrans, four years after his death in Los Angeles. Pauncefote died on May 24, 1902, and his order belongs to May 27. It lowers the flags "on Wednesday, [Mar.] 28, 1902, the day of the funeral"; May 28 was a Wednesday, and wire reports put his national funeral at St. John's Church that day. The "Mar." printed in Richardson, and copied by Lord and the American Presidency Project, is a misprint for "May."

## Historical context
These orders trace the outlines of the moment. Land offices opened, merged, and closed as the West filled. The new island possessions needed lighthouses, quarantine stations, coaling depots, and naval ground, and a modern fleet needed rules for pay and discharge. The Sharretts appointment ties the new administration to the aftermath of the Boxer crisis in China, and the Fort Sill enlargement is a reminder that Geronimo's people were still prisoners of war.

## Political impact
None of these orders drew recorded press attention. They were the ordinary paperwork of a working presidency, and they mattered to the land district, the post, the sailor, or the clerk they named.

## Legacy
The order of August 9, 1902 set aside about 240 acres in the Nebraska Sand Hills "for the use of the Department of Agriculture for purposes in connection with experimental tree planting." That ground became the Bessey Nursery. It was a companion to the Nebraska National Forest, not its origin: the Dismal River and Niobrara Forest Reserves had been proclaimed four months earlier and took the national-forest name in 1908.

The naval order of June 13, 1902 on Adak and Kiska, which Lord spells "Adakh and Kyska," anticipated the strategic weight those Aleutian islands would carry four decades later.
